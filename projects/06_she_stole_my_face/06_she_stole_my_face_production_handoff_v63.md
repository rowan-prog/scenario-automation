## 1. Use Of Script

Please treat the script as the story baseline: plot order, relationship turns, core dialogue intent, continuity, and episode hooks come from the locked script.

Production can adjust staging, camera, blocking, pacing, cuts, performance detail, and visual detail when it makes the scene play stronger. The key is to preserve the same story result and emotional result.

Dialogue may be performed as written. If a line plays clearer, sharper, or more natural in spoken North American vertical-drama English, a production-side alternative can be proposed. Please avoid line changes that create new plot logic, new props, new proof, or procedural explanation.

## 2. Story Core

Keep the read simple and emotional:

- Lena is the real Lena Sterling.
- Mara stole Lena's face, name, home, fiancé, and public life.
- Public scenes work best when Mara is believed and Lena is humiliated.
- Lena wins by stopping the screaming, watching Mara take too much, and letting Mara expose herself in public.
- Noah protects Lena by choice and instinct. He should not turn into a detective.
- Ethan's regret comes too late. He is not redeemed.
- The climax should feel like public collapse: Mara loses the room, Eileen gives her up, Ethan breaks, and Lena takes the pendant back.

## 3. What To Protect

- Lena and Mara have the same face. The audience should tell them apart through pendant, styling, wounds, behavior, and social position.
- The gold pendant is the main prop: Lena starts with it, Mara steals and wears it, Lena reclaims it at the finale.
- The story should not become a police, DNA, courtroom, medical-record, CCTV, boardroom, or evidence-hunt drama.
- Phones, TV, livestreams, and chyrons are for public shame and public reversal, not for solving the case.
- Mara should stay greedy, cruel, and unstable under pressure. She is not a tragic genius.
- Lena should stay hurt, dignified, and increasingly steady. She is not a smug mastermind.

## 4. Character Read

**Lena Sterling**  
Warm, dignified, wounded, but not weak. Her victory should feel earned through survival and steadiness.

**Mara Voss**  
Sweet in public, filthy and cruel in private. She performs grief and victimhood well enough for people to believe her until pressure breaks her.

**Noah Keene**  
Quiet power. Protective, romantic, and certain. He believes Lena without turning the story into an investigation.

**Ethan Cross**  
Handsome, proud, cruel, and late. He feels something around the real Lena but chooses pride until the truth destroys him.

**Eileen Voss**  
Cold, practical, self-protective. Her climax betrayal is survival, not conscience.

**Victoria Cross**  
Polished class cruelty. She treats Lena like dirt, not like an equal enemy.

**Margaret / Woman in Pearls**  
Helena's old friend. She gives Lena one public emotional opening, then caves under pressure. She is not an evidence witness.

## 5. Visual And Tone

The show should look attractive, legible, and emotionally loud: stolen luxury, public shame, sexual betrayal, and clean revenge payoff.

Keep the leads camera-friendly and genre-appropriate. Lena/Mara should read beautiful but emotionally accessible. Noah and Ethan should read wealthy, handsome, and romantic-drama desirable, with Noah darker and more dangerous, Ethan softer and blonder.

Avoid prestige-drama restraint, detective-show staging, and over-explained procedural visuals. The emotional read should stay immediate.
